# CalkCheck Audit — calks.uk — 15 August 2026

**Locale:** en-GB · GBP · gov.uk / hmrc.gov.uk / nhs.uk / ons.gov.uk / fca.org.uk
**Rounds:** three passes in one cycle. Round 1 sampled 20 pages and found nothing.
Rounds 2 and 3 added the **CALC-10 marginal-scale** check and found 25 real defects.

## Headline

The automated surface is genuinely clean and has been for three runs. Everything
of value this cycle came from one check the scripts cannot perform: **recomputing
a progressive scale by hand and comparing.**

| Surface | Result |

## Round 2 — the basic-rate band (17 calculators)

The 20% band was pinned to a £50,270 *gross* ceiling instead of being a fixed
**£37,700 of taxable income**. As the Personal Allowance tapered above £100,000
the band stretched, taxing at 20% income that belongs at 40%. Understated tax by
up to **£2,514**, and implied a 50% marginal rate in the taper zone instead of
the well-known **60% trap**. Fixed and deployed; verified live.

## Round 3 — the same confusion, everywhere else

Round 2's regex fixed one *shape* of the bug. Round 3 asked where else gross and
taxable thresholds get mixed up, and found the answer was: in allowances, in
dividends, in Corporation Tax, and in the *second* code block of files whose
first block round 2 had already fixed.

### 1. Blind Person's Allowance — two independent defects

**Stale rate.** £3,130 in the code; the 2026/27 figure is **£3,250**. The value
had *already been corrected in the constants ledger* by an earlier freshness
pass — but never applied to the `.tsx`, so ledger and live site disagreed for
months. The in-code comment justified £3,130 by deriving it from a 1.7% CPI
uprating; 1.7% was the Sep-2024 CPI, which produced last year's number.

**Halved saving.** The calculator capped the 20% band at (£50,270 − allowance),
shrinking the basic-rate band by exactly the allowance. An allowance *lifts* the
band structure, it does not compress it. A higher-rate taxpayer saw **£626
instead of £1,300** — half the actual benefit, on a page whose entire purpose is
showing that benefit. It had no 45% band at all.

The page's own prose said £1,228 / £614 — themselves derived from the *older*
£3,070 — and one worked example read `£3,250 × 40% = £1,228`, contradicting its
own arithmetic. Now £650 / £1,300 / £1,462.50 throughout, verified in-browser.

### 2. Sole trader vs limited company — the conclusion was wrong

Both tools charged a **flat 25% Corporation Tax with no marginal relief** across
£50k–£250k (the range owner-managed companies actually occupy) and taxed **every
dividend at the basic 10.75%** with no higher-rate band. Corrected, the answer
moves: at £100,000 profit the sole trader is **£5,302 ahead**, where the tools
had shown roughly break-even.

The supporting prose then contradicted the corrected calculator in five places —
including a comparison table built on a stale Class 4 NI rate and charging
Corporation Tax on profit *before* deducting the salary, concluding "Ltd saves
£3,054" where the truth is the reverse. Rewritten to match, with the modelling
assumption (full annual extraction) now stated.

### 3. Contractor day rate — a 15% under-quote

The outside-IR35 branch assumed a flat 25% uplift over the target take-home
rather than solving for it. To net £60,000 over 220 days the answer is **£405/day,
not £345**. It now solves for the target and lands on it exactly (£60,000.00).

### 4. IR35 — the page had inverted

The inside-IR35 side computed employer NI and never deducted it, while the
outside side did. Once the outside branch was corrected this flipped the page,
which then displayed "**−£4,786.59** more per year outside IR35". Both sides are
now like-for-like; outside is ahead by £1,157 on the defaults.

### 5. Emergency pension tax and tax refunds

A band array of *taxable* income widths contained
`{ rate: 0.40, width: 125_140 - 50_270 }` — a difference of two *gross*
thresholds. That made the 40% band £12,570 too narrow and started the 45% rate
that much early, overstating tax by **£628.50** (and **£377.10** in Scotland,
where the 48% top rate started early for the same reason).

### 6. Second-block misses

`NonDomTaxCalculator` and `SoleTraderVsLtdCalculator` each had **two** tax
blocks. Round 2 fixed the first in each file and left the second — non-dom
worldwide income and sole-trader profit, both of which sit precisely in the
taper zone where the bug bites hardest.

### 7. No additional rate at all

`AgencyWorker`, `NhsPay`, `TeacherPay`, `ZeroHours` and `CostOfLiving` stopped at
40% with no allowance taper and no 45% band. Now on the shared helper.

## Not defects — checked and cleared

- **`DividendIncomeCalculator`** applies a flat basic rate, but says so in the
  visible UI ("Estimated dividend tax (basic rate, outside ISA)"). An honest
  stated assumption is not a stale value (DATA-7).
- **CGT `remainingBasic = 50,270 − otherIncome`** looks like the same bug but is
  algebraically identical to the correct form, and both give zero above £100,000.
- **`£1,228/mo`** in a mortgage LTV table — coincidental digits, unrelated.
- The four standing false positives (sources-coverage script, i18n analyzer,
  EAT-1 on gov.uk sub-domains, `£ £` on money inputs) recurred as expected.

## Verification

## The lesson worth keeping

Three consecutive audits reported "no defects" while a systemic error sat in the
flagship calculators. The scripts verify *format*; they cannot verify *method*.
Every real finding in this cycle came from picking a value in the middle of a
band and recomputing it by hand.

Two second-order lessons: after fixing one instance, grep for the *shape* of the
bug rather than the exact string (round 2's regex missed four variants and two
second blocks); and when a fix changes a calculator's answer, the surrounding
prose was written against the old answer and must be re-read.
