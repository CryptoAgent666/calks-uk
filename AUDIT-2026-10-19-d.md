## Headline

**154 defects on 20/20 pages (9 HIGH, 56 MED, 89 LOW); 9 calculators gave wrong answers for realistic inputs and 8 more
described inputs, modes or examples the tool could not produce.** The worst were on pages that looked authoritative:

- The **VAT** rate table put sanitary products at 5%, although they have been zero-rated since January 2021. It also put
  domestic energy at 5%, although household electricity in Great Britain carries no VAT from 1 October 2026 to 31 March 2027.
  Its "Reverse VAT" button ran the same code as "Remove VAT".
- The **pension annual allowance** tool estimated threshold income without adding back salary sacrifice, which FA 2004
  s.228ZA(5) requires. So a £280k earner sacrificing £90k was cleared of the taper and shown a £13,500 charge instead
  of £18,000. It also taxed the whole excess at one rate picked from adjusted income: a basic-rate MPAA case was charged
  £800 instead of £400.
- The **mortgage early repayment** tool counted all remaining interest as a "saving" (default "Save £89,704.53"). It
  ignored what the cash earns in the meantime, and the user can repay for free once the charge ends. At its own defaults,
  waiting is £2,711 better.
- The **standing charge** tool assumed a zero standing charge with a fixed +2.5p, and its consumption was fixed. What
  actually exists in 2026 is Ofgem's one-year lower standing charge pilot (since June 2026). On its terms a typical home
  pays about £32.50 more, not £8.46 less. The regulatory history on the page was also wrong.
- The **home insurance** estimate came out at £175.50 for a typical semi, against the ABI's £383 average premium paid
  (Q2 2026). The page said switching at renewal saves 30–50% because of auto-renewal hikes, which FCA rules have banned
  since January 2022. It also said a new-build NHBC warranty means no buildings cover is needed for 0–3 years. That is
  wrong and risky.
- **Moneyfacts mortgage averages** quoted the mid-September 5.73% / 5.78% on 15 pages and in three calculator defaults.
  The 19 October release has 5.93% / 5.95% at the start of October. The Mortgage Guarantee Scheme was still described as
  "extended to June 2026, to be replaced by Freedom to Buy", although it has been permanent since July 2025.

Confirmed current at source (no change needed): employer NI 15% over £5,000 (£96 a week / £417 a month), Employment

## Method notes

- Sample: computed from the slug list against every round report, not from memory notes. A word-level grep found ~25
  "mentions" that were sweep fixes, not hand-checks.
- Pipeline:
  - Four read-only reviewers, five pages each, with at least two hand-worked inputs per calculator.
  - The lead re-verified each HIGH at source: gov.uk VAT rates page and the "Breathing space" note, Moneyfacts 19 Oct
    release, Ofgem pilot page, ABI tracker report, MGS 2025 rules.
  - An editor for each review started as soon as it landed. A fifth editor did the site-wide mortgage-rate sweep. All
    ran Edit-only on the shared data files in one checkout, with no clobbering across 30 files.
  - Lead sweep of out-of-sample copies, then guards tested both ways, ledger, build, and Playwright on dist with ads
    blocked.
- The parse command in the editor rules (`esbuild --loader=ts <file>`) fails on this esbuild version. Use
  `npx esbuild <file> --log-level=error`.
- The gov.uk "rates of VAT on different goods" page still shows 5% for domestic electricity. The 0% period comes from the
  26 Aug 2026 announcement, so a page-level gov.uk check alone would have "confirmed" the stale value.
